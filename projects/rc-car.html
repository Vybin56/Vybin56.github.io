Wireless RC Car and Electronic Steering Differential

A ground up RC platform built to test drive control ideas: a printed chassis, two independently driven rear wheels, and a differential algorithm that trims wheel speed through a corner instead of fighting it.

Why a differential

A fixed rear axle forces both wheels to turn at the same speed. In a corner the inside wheel travels a shorter arc than the outside one, so one of the two has to slip. On a light RC chassis that slip shows up as understeer and lost traction on corner exit.

The electronic differential removes the constraint. Each rear wheel gets its own motor, and the firmware computes a speed offset from the steering input so the two wheels turn at the speeds the geometry is actually asking for.

Control path

From thumbstick to PWM in as few steps as possible.

  • A PS5 controller pairs to a Python interface that translates stick positions into throttle and steering commands.
  • Commands stream wirelessly to embedded C++ firmware on the Arduino, which owns PWM timing and the differential calculation.
  • The loop was kept deliberately short so the car responds without the lag that makes a remote vehicle undriveable.

Power and motor hardware

  • An 11.1 V 3S LiPo is the single source for the whole car.
  • Dual buck converters step it down into separate supplies so logic and actuators do not share a rail.
  • An L298N dual H bridge drives both rear motors, with direction and PWM on separate pins so each side can be commanded independently.
  • The steering servo runs off its own supply, so its stall current cannot brown out the microcontroller mid corner.

Chassis

The chassis was designed in CAD and 3D printed, with modular mounting enclosures for the dual DC drive motors, the steering servo and the control electronics. Printing it meant the mounting geometry could be revised between test sessions instead of being worked around.

Demonstration

The car was shown in a live technical demonstration to club leadership and project leads, which validated control responsiveness, chassis integrity and firmware stability under real driving rather than on a bench.

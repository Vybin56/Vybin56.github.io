Automated Filament Spooler and AI Recycling System

The electrical half of a closed loop plastic recycling line: it pulls extruded filament through a cooling section and winds it onto a spool at a matched speed, so the filament neither stretches nor piles up.

Role
Power distribution, motor control, current budget

The line

Recycled plastic is extruded into filament, cooled, and wound onto a spool. Two motions have to stay in agreement: the puller that draws filament out of the extruder, and the winder that takes it up.

If the winder runs fast the filament thins and snaps. If it runs slow the filament piles up before the spool. Everything electrical on this project exists to keep those two speeds in a usable ratio.

Power distribution

  • A 12 V distribution network off a 10 A to 12 A supply, feeding three DC motors, dual cooling fans and the Arduino.
  • Current demand was calculated per branch before anything was wired, so the supply was sized to the load rather than the load discovered afterwards.
  • Fans sit on the filament path between the extruder and the puller, so the plastic is set before it is under tension.

Motor control

Dual H bridge drivers control the pulling and winding motors, both commanded from the central Arduino. Driving them independently is what makes the ratio between them adjustable rather than fixed by gearing.

Mechanical integration

Printed brackets, pulleys and shafts carry the spool and the tensioning arm, with the cooling fan mounted on its own frame over the filament path. Interfacing the motor control to that mechanism, sizing the current it draws, and wiring it safely was my half of the project.

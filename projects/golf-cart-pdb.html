Autonomous Golf Cart Power Distribution Board

Takes 24 V from a 100 Ah LiFePO4 traction pack and splits it between a brushed DC motor drive and the compute stack of an autonomous golf cart.

The brief

The AutoPilot team is converting a golf cart into an autonomous vehicle. Its traction pack is 24 V at 100 Ah, and everything downstream of it, the motor drive, a single board computer, four microcontrollers and their sensors, was being wired point to point.

One board now carries motor level current and hands out clean logic rails. On a vehicle that drives itself, loose high current wiring is a safety problem before it is an electrical one.

Power architecture

Three tiers, so each stage solves one problem.

  • The 24 V rail runs straight to the motor driver through its own fuse and bulk capacitance, keeping the highest current path short.
  • A 24 V to 12 V buck feeds everything that is not the drive motor.
  • A 12 V to 5 V stage supplies a Raspberry Pi 5 and four ESP32 controllers.
  • Voltage, current and thermal margin checked on every rail, not just the input.

Carrying the current

Copper sized to a standard, not to a guess.

  • Traces sized to IPC-2221 for a 15 to 20 A continuous target.
  • 2 oz copper on both layers, polygon pours instead of discrete traces.
  • Via stitching so both pours share the load and spread the heat.

Thermal dissipation was a placement constraint from the first layout pass, not something to measure after the board came back.

Protection and inrush

  • 5 A on the logic branch and 20 A on the motor branch, coordinated with the driver's programmable current limit so the fuse acts last.
  • Bulk capacitance at the motor driver input holds the rail up through current steps.
  • Anti spark connectors, so closing the loop on a 100 Ah pack does not weld the contacts.

Handoff

Shipped with a full bill of materials and wiring documentation, which kept component validation, thermal analysis and the design review straightforward rather than archaeological.

What I would do next

Current sense on each branch, reported back to the compute stack, so the vehicle can see its own power draw instead of waiting for a fuse.
